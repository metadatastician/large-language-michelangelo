= Roadmap
:toc:

Planned, not scheduled. v0.1–v0.3 (all shipped 2026-08-31) are the
demonstrator: deterministic voxel forms, carved and grown on one timeline,
with instruments that report the engine rather than the script.

== 1 · The maths in motion (honesty instrumentation) — SHIPPED 2026-08-31

A live readout beside each pane showing the *actual* variables that drive the
render, so the construction difference is evidence, not narration:

* Sculptor: `wasteMesh.count = N − k` ticking *down*; the form mesh shown as
  `always Nt — occluded`.
* Gardener: `gardenMesh.count = k` ticking *up* from zero.
* Per-pane `renderer.info.render.triangles` — engine-reported, not scripted:
  the left number falls, the right number rises, and they meet.
* A scrolling operation tape: `− stroke №12,004 @ (x,y,z)` / `+ voxel №1,833
  @ …`, append-only on both sides.

== 2 · Many forms, one machinery — SHIPPED 2026-08-31

The carve side was already form-agnostic (BFS distance from any target set
orders the waste); the generator is now form-agnostic too:

* `FORMS` registry — each form contributes only `fill(seed, rnd, noise,
  type, col, gkey)`: its own cells, colours, and growth key. Marble, carve
  ordering, count-scrub, instruments and verifier are shared.
* Shipped forms: tree (branch skeleton), torus knot (seed-picked (p,q),
  growth key threads the curve), letter M (hand-authored bitmap — canvas
  text metrics differ per OS and would break same-seed-same-voxels),
  chess rook (lathe profile, growth key spirals like a lathe pass),
  castle (keep/towers/walls, growth key = masonry courses).
* Per-form colour bootstrap: 1-bit threshold and 4-colour palette are
  luminance quartiles of the chosen form's finished colouring.

== 3 · Prompt-driven forms — v0.2 SHIPPED 2026-09-14, v0.1/v0.3 outstanding

The two pathways can only consume a *fixed form specification* — the
invariant requires the form to exist before either pathway starts. So a
prompt pipeline is a **form compiler** in front of unchanged machinery.

* *v0.1 — picker+params:* prompt → keyword match → parametric family + seed.
  Deterministic, offline, narrow.
* *v0.2 — LLM-compiled spec:* **SHIPPED as `studio.html`, v0.4.0.** The page
  asks Claude (the artifact `sample` capability) to compile a prompt into a
  compact JSON *recipe* — parts drawn from three primitives in normalised
  coordinates — which the page rasterises deterministically into the block and
  both pathways then construct, unchanged. Produces real composed scenes; weak
  likeness for named characters, which is the honest ceiling of a 27×27×40
  block rather than a filter. The recipe, not the prompt, is the interface: a
  JSON editor and an example library work with no model present, so the Pages
  copy is fully usable offline. Revision is edit-and-diff — a follow-up prompt
  edits the retained recipe and the page reports the changed parts and fields
  alongside the exact count of voxels added, removed and recoloured.
* *v0.3 — reference-image mode:* any raster (image-model output or an upload)
  quantised into cells/stroke-groups; carve and growth orders computed
  generically. Fidelity is delegated to the reference; this machinery
  contributes dual construction and addressability. This is the tier where
  "an established character, but set in the ancient world" actually
  resembles the character.
* In-artifact SLM honestly assessed: the artifact CSP blocks runtime weight
  fetches and the 16 MB budget excludes useful models. Local repo mode has
  no such sandbox — ONNX/WebGPU SLMs are viable there, not in the artifact.

*Proof obligations attached to this item:* P-5 is the gate — a compiled form
is admissible only if it arrives with a checkable partition witness, so the
compiler can be wrong about likeness but never about the invariant. As shipped,
`studio.html` satisfies P-5's live half for free: `verifyInvariant()` re-derives
the partition from the buffers a compiled recipe produced, exactly as it does
for a hand-coded form. Note the limit honestly — the verifier proves the
partition, not the sculpture, and passes on a degenerate fill where nothing is
form and everything is waste. Emptiness and over-fill are therefore refused by
the compiler ahead of the build, as usability guards, not as proof. P-4
(interleaving equivalence) becomes load-bearing here too: compiled scenes
will want layered/streamed construction orders, and the claim that any
interleaving of the two op streams reaches the same final frame must be
proved, not assumed.

== 4 · The research programme (the point of all this) — uncommissioned

* *Addressable revision experiment:* "remove stroke-group N" vs chat-loop
  image revision — measure whether procedural addressability eliminates the
  basin-anchored, unverifiable-revision failure class.
* *Cross-pathway error taxonomy:* where do carve-order and growth-order
  disagree or produce artefacts a single pathway hides?
* *Pathway specialisation:* which demands suit selection-from-abundance,
  which suit construction-from-seed; can the two co-evolve as skills?
* *Diffusion tie-in:* a third pane replaying a deterministic (DDIM) denoising
  trajectory — the sculptor that industry already ships without saying so.

*Proof obligations attached to this item:* the experiment is only meaningful
over machinery whose invariants are mechanised (P-6), and the diffusion pane
inherits P-7 — the claim "deterministic sampler ⇒ the noise block uniquely
determines the image" must be stated precisely (ODE-flow uniqueness under
Lipschitz conditions) rather than gestured at.

== 5 · Research increments — planned 2026-09-14

Small, independently valuable steps, each of which converts a stated claim into
a checked one, or makes a checked claim citable. Ordered by value per hour, not
by dependency. The organising rule: *every increment should either make a claim
checkable, or make a checkable claim citable.*

* *R1 — Golden determinism test.* **SHIPPED in v0.4.0**; see P-7. Retires the
  repository's zero-test status.
* *R2 — P-4 interleaving equivalence, in two stages.* First as a property test
  over R1's harness — shuffle the removal order N times and assert the final
  buffers are byte-identical — then as the mechanised proof that
  `block ∖ removeAll = addAll` over a finite cell set, independent of order.
  Evidence first, proof second; runtime checking is not proof.
* *R3 — Model-tier evaluation.* Run one prompt set across the `quick`,
  `default` and `complex` sampling tiers and measure recipe validity rate, part
  count, fill fraction and invariant pass. This answers with data the question
  that prompted §3 — *does a small, narrow model suffice?* — and reports n and
  failure modes, not a headline rate.
* *R4 — Revision-transcript export.* Export a studio session: each revision's
  prompt, the recipe diff, and the voxel add/remove/recolour counts. This is
  the dataset §4's addressable-revision experiment needs; without it that item
  stays an essay rather than an experiment.
* *R5 — Cross-pathway error taxonomy via connectivity.* Measure per-frame
  connectivity of the partial form along the shared timeline. The asymmetry is
  real and measurable: a Gardener partial can be disconnected — floating voxels
  awaiting their support — where a Sculptor partial cannot be disconnected in
  the same way. A finding about the two metaphysics, not decoration.
* *R6 — P-6 monotonicity mechanised*, once R2 establishes the pattern.

Exploratory, worth trying and not yet committed: the diffusion pane (§4, the
largest single addition and the strongest); reference-image mode (§3 v0.3); and
a local ONNX/WebGPU small model, which the artifact CSP forbids but local repo
mode permits.

*Mechanisation target, decided 2026-09-14:* **Idris2**. The discriminator is
re-runnability — the estate already ships `idris2-abi-check` CI tooling, so a
proof lands inside a gate rather than beside one.

== 6 · Citation — planned 2026-09-14

The project is research as much as demonstration, so each increment should be
citable as well as checkable. Retro-tag v0.3.0, deposit via the Zenodo↔GitHub
integration, and write the resulting DOI back into `CITATION.cff` and
`README.adoc`. Zenodo mints a concept DOI spanning every version alongside each
version DOI, so release cadence and research increments align from here on: a
small step that is worth making is worth citing. Scope is the software plus a
short technical note, linked under the one concept DOI.

== P · Proof obligations

The page's claims, split into what is *checked live* on every build and what
remains *to be done* as mechanised proof. Runtime checking is not proof and
is not claimed as such (see `rsr-profile.a2ml`).

.Checked live, in-page (`llmProof.verify()`, verdict line under the timeline)
* *P-1 Disjointness* — the removal stream and the addition stream share no
  cell, and neither stream repeats a cell.
* *P-2 Completeness* — every cell of the block appears in exactly one
  stream: `Nt + Nw = NCELL`, re-counted from the live buffers.
* *P-3 Convergence* — the addition stream is exactly the typed form; the
  removal stream is exactly the untyped waste. Hence final-frame equality of
  the two panes: what the Sculptor never removes ≡ what the Gardener adds.

.To be done (mechanised, target Idris2 or Lean; roadmap only, no code yet)
* *P-4 Interleaving equivalence* — for any monotone interleaving of the two
  op streams, the final frame is invariant; the timeline's `k(t)` is one
  path through a lattice whose sinks all agree. (Finite-set statement:
  `block ∖ removeAll = addAll` independent of order.)
* *P-5 Compiled-form admissibility* — a form compiler (roadmap §3) must emit
  a witness that its output is a partition of the block; the checker, not
  the compiler, is trusted.
* *P-6 Monotonicity* — the Sculptor's drawn-count is non-increasing and the
  Gardener's non-decreasing along the timeline; scrubbing backwards is
  re-selection, not un-removal, and the formal statement should say so.
* *P-7 Determinism of the definition* — `generateSpecimen : Seed → Form →
  Spec` is a function (no ambient state). Evidence, as of v0.4.0, is a golden
  test rather than a claim: `test/determinism.test.mjs` runs the engine core
  headlessly under `node --test` with no Three.js present, hashing the `type`,
  `col` and `gkey` buffers for each (form, seed) pair against the 12
  fingerprints committed in `test/golden.json`. The fingerprints were minted on
  Node 26.5.0 and reproduce byte-identically on CI's Node 22.23.2 — two
  machines and two major runtime versions — and the suite is gated on every
  push. This discharges the *evidence* clause only; proof by construction over
  a pure PRNG remains outstanding, and a passing golden test is not that.
